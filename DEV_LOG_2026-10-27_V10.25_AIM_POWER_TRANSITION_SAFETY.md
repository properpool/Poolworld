# Pool World v10.25 — aim/power input transaction safety

Date: 2026-10-27 UTC. Branch: `development/poolworld-match-engine`. **Not a production release.**

## User-visible defect and reproducible baseline

A NEW RACK or UNDO performed while an aiming finger or power thumb was still held did not retire the captured gesture. Its later pointerup could rotate the cue in a new/restored rack, or auto-fire a shot after resetting or undoing. A two-finger shot queued while the aiming finger remained down could also leave a stale aim/power selection after NEW RACK. These are genuine match-control faults, distinct from the physical Samsung Galaxy A37's unverified weak/jerky feel.

New mandatory real-Chromium test `tests/test_pool60_gesture_transition.py` exercises reset/undo during aiming, reset/undo during power pulls, queued two-finger reset, and subsequent deliberate SHOOT across 360×800, 390×844, 844×390, and 768×1024, including BIG TABLE on both portrait phones. The deliberately failing baseline CI [#451](https://github.com/properpool/Poolworld/actions/runs/40541330885) reproduced **30 failures of 120 checks** (90/120). Examples: a reset power pull could fire the fresh rack with cue speed ~9.77, and stale pointerup changed restored aim. Job and decoded log inspected.

## Fix

`abandonActiveShotGestures()` retires the aim and power pointer owners, clears drag and queued-shot state, and releases pointer capture only after ownership is cleared. Called from both `rack()` and `restore()`, alongside the existing cue-ball placement cancellation. The reset/undo transaction is now authoritative, so late pointermove/up/cancel/lostpointercapture events cannot mutate its match state. Normal aiming, relative power drag, two-finger intentional shots, and manual SHOOT are retained.
