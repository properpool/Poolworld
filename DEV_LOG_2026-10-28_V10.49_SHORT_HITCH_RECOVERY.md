# Pool World v10.49 — isolated 260–340ms mobile shot-hitch recovery

Date: 28 October 2026 (UTC). Development branch only: `development/poolworld-match-engine`.

## Reproduced user-visible defect
A single 260–340ms animation scheduling interruption after normal 60Hz gameplay was incorrectly charged as moving-shot physics time. On the unchanged baseline, the returning callback ran eight 120Hz ticks immediately and carried ~200–280ms of physics debt into subsequent normal frames. At 75% selected power, one 300ms fixture advanced the cue ball by 25.78 table units on the returning callback and a further 25.48 units on the next 60Hz callback, creating a pronounced lurch. A 25% fixture also jumped ~10.16 then ~9.90 units.

Mandatory new `tests/test_pool60_short_hitch.py` exercises 260/300/340ms interruptions at 25% and 75% power, two warmup lengths and four mobile/tablet viewports, plus sustained 3fps and 3.5fps recovery after smooth gameplay. It verifies frozen physics/visual pose during an isolated hitch, zero stale debt, preserved calibrated shot impulse, normal next-frame motion, bounded sustained recovery and absence of browser exceptions.

- Staging candidate CI #585: **success, 268/268 new checks and all existing mandatory suites passing**. Jobs and decoded logs inspected.

The frame scheduler now recognises an isolated 250–350ms interruption only after a genuinely smooth callback. It holds the moving balls at their last verified pose instead of fast-forwarding. If the following callback is also slow, deferred time is recovered through the existing bounded sustained-low-FPS budget; if normal, the interruption is discarded. This preserves ongoing 3–3.5fps physics and does not change the 120Hz physics tick, calibrated shot power, collision geometry, scoring or visual assets.
