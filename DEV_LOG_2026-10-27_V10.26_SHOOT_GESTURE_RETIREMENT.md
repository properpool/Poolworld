# Pool World v10.26 — committed SHOOT retires old touchscreen gestures

Date: 2026-10-27 UTC. Branch: `development/poolworld-match-engine`. **Development preview only; not a production release.**

## Reproducible user-visible defect

Pressing SHOOT while an aiming finger or power thumb remained held started the physical shot without retiring that old captured gesture. If the finger was released after the shot settled, its stale pointerup could change the next turn's aim/power or trigger a second unsolicited shot. In a 360×800 Chromium touchscreen fixture, an explicit 100% power shot was followed by a late power-finger release that launched another 9.7695-speed shot and changed the selected power to 45%. An old aiming finger also rotated the next turn after release. This is a real match-control defect; it is **not** a verified physical Samsung Galaxy A37 shot-feel improvement.

## Mandatory reproducing regression

Added `tests/test_pool60_shoot_gesture_retirement.py` and registered it in `.github/workflows/poolworld-staging-tests.yml`. It exercises real Chromium touchscreen pointer ownership on 360×800, 390×844, 844×390 and 768×1024, including portrait BIG TABLE mode. It tests a held aiming finger, a held power thumb and a two-finger queued shot while an explicit DOM SHOOT command commits a shot. It asserts immediate gesture retirement, preserved calibrated initial cue impulse, no late cue rotation, no power drift, no double shot and no browser exceptions. A synthetic completed-shot state is used to exercise the late-release boundary without waiting for a whole physical match; these are **browser regression fixtures**, not physical-device acceptance.
