# Pool World v10.32 — sustained 3–4fps shot-time preservation (28 October 2026)

**Scope:** development branch only. Production `main/index.html` must not be changed without independent physical Samsung/human acceptance and explicit user approval.

## Reproduced gameplay defect
At synthetic 3fps the old 120Hz accumulator completed only 652 of the 960 required physics steps across 24 callbacks; at 3.5fps it completed 652 of approximately 823, and at 4fps 652 of 720. The time cap and 28-step limit made real cue-ball motion slow down in low-frame-rate browsers. In a four-second 3.5fps cue-only shot, the old position was x=763.73 versus the 120Hz reference x=637.91, with different velocity. These are synthetic Chromium timings, not observed Samsung A37 frame rates.

## Reproduction and correction
Added mandatory `tests/test_pool60_threefps_motion.py` and workflow registration, covering four viewports, 3/3.5/4/4.5/5/6/15/60fps, real cue travel/velocity, and isolated 180–400ms hitches. The deliberately failing baseline workflow #496 (run 40609584552) passed 92/124, with 32 genuine failures; completed job and decoded logs inspected.

Staging allows a maximum 350ms callback interval only when that interval actually occurs, with sustained slow-frame detection up to 350ms, at most 750ms accumulated debt after two consecutive slow frames, and a maximum 44 physics ticks for sustained 3–4fps frames. The first isolated stall remains limited to eight ticks. A raw gap greater than 350ms resets the slow-frame detector and retains the eight-tick safety limit. The later 500ms interruption-after-sustained-slow test prevents an unbounded catch-up burst. 5–15fps step budgets, calibrated shot impulse, six-pocket physics, rules, visuals, and four original approved audio data URIs are preserved.
