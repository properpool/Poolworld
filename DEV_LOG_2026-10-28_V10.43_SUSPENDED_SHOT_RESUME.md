# Pool World v10.43 — shot-motion continuity after mobile tab suspension (28 October 2026)

## User-visible defect
When an active shot was interrupted by a long browser/phone suspension, the next animation callback advanced eight 120Hz physics ticks immediately and left roughly 183–192ms of simulation debt. The following 60Hz callbacks could each run another eight ticks, producing repeated cue-ball jumps rather than smoothly resuming play. This differs from ordinary sustained low FPS: the browser was silent for 1.2, 3 or 10 seconds.

The unchanged 75%-power baseline at 768×1024 after a 3-second suspension moved the cue ball **25.85 table pixels on the first returning frame**, then **25.55 more pixels on the next 16.7ms frame**, while still carrying 142ms of stale debt. The baseline also produced 10.45px and 10.19px successive jumps at 25% power. These are reproducible browser simulations, **not measured Samsung Galaxy A37 behaviour**.

## Correction and promotion
A >1-second gap during an already-active shot is treated as suspension rather than simulation time. The engine drops only the suspended interval's stale physics debt, preserves the live ball velocities and positions, and aligns the previous-position and rotation interpolation buffers to the authoritative current ball poses. The first resumed frame therefore does not teleport or visually snap the ball backward; subsequent 60Hz frames continue normally. A genuinely new shot fired within 50ms of the resumed callback still receives its legitimate post-input simulation time.

The ordinary 60–144Hz cadence, 3–15fps sustained-low-FPS catch-up, bounded 100–500ms transient hitch handling, calibrated shot power, six-pocket collision and match rules, and four original approved v3.9 audio URI bytes remain unchanged.

## Limitations and release blocker
These are deterministic Chromium browser timing/physics/render checks. They do **not** establish that the physical Samsung Galaxy A37 actually suspends in this manner, runs smoothly, has satisfactory shot power or graphics, or meets human gameplay acceptance. The user's reported weak/jerky shots remain unresolved on-device; independent physical Samsung/human acceptance is still **release-blocking**. Do not change production without explicit user approval and physical acceptance. Continue prioritising meaningful reproducible ball-motion, shot-strength, touch and match-rules defects rather than cosmetic micro-optimizations.
