## Reproduced defect

The v9.7 120 Hz physics scheduler correctly retained time from a transient 150 ms frame hitch, but under **sustained** low refresh (80 consecutive 100 ms frame gaps, 10 fps) it allowed the physics accumulator to grow without limit while processing at most eight physics ticks per callback.

The mandatory new `tests/test_pool60_sustained_frame_debt.py` suite reproduced this across four viewport sizes (390×844, 844×390, 768×1024, 1366×768). The baseline GitHub Actions run **#281** failed **8 of 64** new checks: in each viewport, accumulated debt grew to **2.6667 seconds** after the prolonged 10 fps sequence, and remained **1.1667 seconds** after 30 recovery frames at 60 fps. This stale debt could cause accelerated catch-up after a slow device recovers. The old 150 ms transient-hitch tests continued to pass.

## Gameplay fix

Candidate commit `014bcbaee0b63438410bfc01222b70eff4b689bb`, staging HTML blob `a29eb92a2ba785131dfce06a25cab8ad25eadc44`:

- Limit the active-shot physics accumulator to **250 ms**, while preserving the existing 250 ms per-callback elapsed-time cap and the maximum of **eight 120 Hz physics steps per animation callback**.
- Preserve the prior transient 150 ms hitch recovery and continue resetting debt while idle.
- Avoid a multi-second catch-up queue after sustained 10 fps rendering. Under sustained low frame rates, simulation necessarily cannot match wall-clock time with an eight-step budget; this deliberately drops *old* excess debt rather than fast-forwarding the cue ball on recovery.
