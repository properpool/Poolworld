# Pool World development log — 28 October 2026 — v10.57 hitch-tail burst safety

## Reproduced player-visible defect
After smooth 60Hz cue-ball movement, an isolated 180–340ms browser animation interruption was correctly frozen. However, if the next callback took 70–125ms (a single slower callback, not evidence of sustained 3–5fps playback), the scheduler repaid the **entire frozen pause** alongside that callback. This caused an immediate multi-dozen-tick simulation burst, producing an abrupt cue-ball leap and excess carried physics debt. The problem could occur at both 25% and 75% calibrated shot power.

## Mandatory regression
Added `tests/test_pool60_hitch_tail_burst.py` and registered it in `.github/workflows/poolworld-staging-tests.yml`. The test runs **644 Chromium assertions** across 360×800, 390×844, 844×390 and 768×1024. It covers four isolated gap lengths (180/220/280/340ms), four slower follow-up callbacks (70/85/100/125ms), and two power settings (25/75%). Checks include no fast-forward on the frozen callback, bounded physics ticks and ball travel on the next callback, no replayed simulation debt, ordinary-frame recovery, unchanged shot impulse, finite rendered positions and no browser exceptions.

## Corrected gameplay
In the animation scheduler, a frozen 180–350ms pause is repaid only when the immediately following callback is also in the **180–350ms sustained-low-FPS range**. A lone 70–125ms follow-up callback advances only its own elapsed time through the existing bounded physics budget. Consecutive genuinely slow 3–5fps callbacks still repay deferred time; ordinary shot-speed calibration, physics impulses, six-pocket rules, match logic, visual assets and all four approved audio URIs are unchanged.
