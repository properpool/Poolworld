# Pool World v10.16 — 13.5–14.5fps shot-speed preservation

Date: 27 October 2026 (UTC). Scope: development/poolworld-match-engine ONLY.

## Reproduced user-visible gameplay defect

The fixed 120Hz simulation requires 8.28–8.89 physics ticks per displayed
frame at sustained 14.5–13.5fps. The former scheduler allowed more than eight
ticks only after two consecutive frame gaps >=75ms. The 69–74ms range never
qualified, so the engine advanced just eight ticks each callback and banked
or discarded simulation time. This makes shots roll too slowly in wall time,
even though each individual physics tick is correct.

On the unchanged baseline, the new mandatory Chromium regression suite
failed 40 of 112 checks across 390x844, 844x390, 768x1024 and 1366x768.
Measured two-second 65% cue shots on each viewport:

| Sustained callback rate | Baseline cue X | Direct 120Hz reference X | Shortfall |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 13.5fps | 914.83 | 958.84 | 44.01 table units |
| 14fps | 929.79 | 958.84 | 29.06 table units |
| 14.5fps | 944.46 | 958.84 | 14.39 table units |

The failure was measured by actual physics motion, not merely an expected
tick-count calculation. These are synthetic Chromium callback rates, not
measurements of the user's Samsung Galaxy A37.

## Correction

Changed sustained-low-FPS detection in the staging gameplay engine from
75ms to 64ms, retaining the existing two-consecutive-frame guard and the
22-step maximum catch-up budget. Isolated 65–74ms hitches still receive
the ordinary eight-tick budget. No shot impulse, friction, collisions,
pockets, aiming, sound, graphics or match rules were changed.

New mandatory test: `tests/test_pool60_14fps_motion.py`, registered in
`.github/workflows/poolworld-staging-tests.yml`. Its 112 checks cover
13.5, 14, 14.5, 15 and 60fps fixed-tick cadence and debt; genuine cue
travel and velocity versus 120Hz reference; isolated 65/70/74ms stalls;
bounded catch-up and browser exceptions across four viewports.

## Release gate and next work

This removes a real slow-motion defect in the narrow 13.5–14.5fps band,
but does NOT improve display smoothness at genuinely low FPS, prove a
Samsung performance improvement, or resolve reported weak-feeling shots,
jerky visuals, difficult aiming or unsatisfactory presentation. Independent
physical Samsung/human acceptance is still mandatory and release-blocking;
production requires explicit owner approval. Do not ask the owner to test
unfinished builds. Prioritise substantial normal-frame-rate gameplay and
presentation defects next. No monetisation, multiplayer or expansion.
