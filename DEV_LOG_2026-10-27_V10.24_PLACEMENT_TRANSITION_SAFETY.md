# Pool World v10.24 — Foul cue-ball placement state-transition safety

Date: 27 October 2026 (UTC). Scope: `development/poolworld-match-engine` only.

## Reproduced user-visible match-state corruption

After a foul, the player can drag the cue ball using a captured touch. Before this fix, pressing **NEW RACK**, **UNDO**, or **PLACE CUE** while the placement finger remained down left the old `placementPointer` active. A later movement and release from that stale finger could:

- move the cue ball in a freshly reset rack to an unrelated location;
- overwrite the restored cue-ball position and end the restored ball-in-hand turn after UNDO;
- reposition the cue ball **after** the player explicitly confirmed its placement.

This is a reproducible match-state and touchscreen safety defect, not a claim about perceived shot power or frame-rate smoothness.

## Mandatory browser regression

It covers 360×800, 390×844, 844×390 and 768×1024, including the rotated BIG TABLE mode on both supported portrait phones: **72 checks across six viewport/layout combinations**.

## Fix and preserved invariants

A single `abandonCuePlacementGesture()` retires the captured placement pointer, clears its origin and safely releases pointer capture before NEW RACK or UNDO changes match state, and when PLACE CUE confirms the previewed position. The old touch's late events are ignored. Ordinary drag-to-place, tap-to-place, cancellation and illegal-drop handling remain unchanged.

The staging and development-preview `index.html` files were independently fetched and confirmed byte-identical at Git blob `824d3c4585a9b70067fdb1f9809da716b2623c60`. Production `main/index.html` remains **untouched**, blob `011657038a72ff255297ca78f595eef0e67df1e3`. All four approved v3.9 audio data URIs remain byte-identical. Shot impulse, fixed-timestep physics, six pockets, match rules and visual assets were not changed by this correction.

## Release restriction

All checks are Chromium browser tests, **not** physical Samsung Galaxy A37 testing, independent human acceptance, or evidence that weak/jerky shots and visual quality have improved. Physical-device and human acceptance plus explicit user approval remain mandatory before any production change. Do not ask the user to test unfinished builds.
