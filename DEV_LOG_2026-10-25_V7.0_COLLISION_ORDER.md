# Pool World v7.0 — clustered collision impulse ordering
Date: 2026-10-25 (UTC). Scope: gameplay physics only.

## Reproduced problem
v6.9 used swept-time first-contact attribution for foul rules but still
resolved overlapping cue/object pairs in nested rack-array order. In the
same legal two-ball staggered fixture, reversing object storage order
produced a maximum state difference of 2.762674 table units after one
120Hz step and 25.317843 after 24 steps, despite identical initial
physical positions. A symmetric double-contact tie could also change
the recorded first-hit ID (1 versus 2) under permutation.

## Fix
- Candidate `POOL_WORLD_6.0_STAGING_MATCH.html` commit
  `ce6afab8c08b42df7f96579b1894a0996970986f`,
  Git blob `8c7cbdee74729a0abd36726d68c0e00f9eb3a475`.
- Gather currently overlapping ball pairs at each 120Hz physics step,
  estimate relative swept circle-contact time, and resolve by that time
  rather than their array indices. Ties have a deterministic ID order.
- For exactly simultaneous cue first-contact ties, prefer lower object
  ID rather than whichever ball appears first in the array.
- Preserve restitution, friction, follow/draw, collision sound events,
  shot rules and approved audio payloads.

## Safeguards and remaining limitations
- Production `main/index.html` remains untouched prototype blob
  `011657038a72ff255297ca78f595eef0e67df1e3`.
- Swept-time sorting is still a sequential impulse approximation:
  perfectly simultaneous multi-ball contact may remain sensitive to
  deterministic tie-breaks, and collision timing within a 120Hz step
  is approximate. Do not claim exact real-world multi-contact physics.
- All acceptance is automated Chromium and scripted physics, not
  physical Samsung/tablet testing or independent human playability.
- Next work: realistic simultaneous-contact solver evaluation, pocket
  jaw/throat near misses, random clustered layouts, imprecise finger
  aiming, full-match stress and ball rolling fidelity.
