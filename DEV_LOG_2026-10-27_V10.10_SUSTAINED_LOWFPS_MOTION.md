# Pool World v10.10 — sustained low-FPS shot-motion correction
Date: 2026-10-27 UTC. Development branch only: `development/poolworld-match-engine`.

## User-reported release blockers
Physical Samsung Galaxy A37 user test: shot power **weak**, ball motion **jerky**, aiming **difficult**, full match **not tested**. These reports remain unresolved as independent physical-device/human acceptance. The work below fixes a reproducible *synthetic sustained-low-FPS slow-motion* defect; it does **not** establish that the physical Samsung has smooth FPS, good aiming, powerful-feeling shots, or a completed match.

## Reproduced defect and mechanism
The fixed-step 120Hz physics engine had an unconditional eight-tick limit per animation callback. A sustained 10fps render cadence owes twelve ticks every 100ms; eight ticks process only two thirds of the simulation. This makes moving balls and shots advance in slow motion, with a stale physics debt capped at 250ms. At 12fps the engine similarly processed only eight of ten required ticks.

Mandatory new Chromium suite `tests/test_pool60_lowfps_motion.py` measures simulated rAF timestamps across 390×844, 844×390, 768×1024 and 1366×768, real cue-ball travel against 120 direct physics ticks, 10/12/15/60fps pacing, one-off 100ms and 150ms hitch safety, and JavaScript errors. It does **not** measure actual physical phone FPS.

The initial baseline CI #355 (run 40447530479) failed **24 of 68** new checks: 10fps produced 160 rather than 240 ticks over 2s; 12fps produced 160 rather than 200. After one synthetic second, cue x was 624.176 versus the correct 120-tick reference x 772.684; velocity 7.614 versus 7.246. All four viewports reproduced the defect. Updated existing sustained-debt regression baseline #356 (run 40447542453) failed four checks (one per viewport): 640 versus at least 950 ticks over eight seconds of 10fps pacing.

## Gameplay fix
At sustained 75–125ms animation intervals, **after two consecutive such intervals**, the scheduler permits enough 120Hz physics ticks for elapsed wall time plus up to two backlog-recovery ticks, strictly capped at fifteen per callback. Isolated 100ms GC/browser stalls and 150–250ms hitches retain the original eight-tick budget, avoiding an unexpected one-frame burst. The cadence detector resets on new rack, new shot and idle. Existing 250ms debt cap and 120Hz physics STEP remain unchanged. No changes to cue impulse curve, pocket geometry, collision restitution, aiming, visual assets, match rules, or approved audio.

An intermediate candidate #357 (run 40447571954) passed all original 68 new checks and existing suites, but its one-frame 100ms interval could use twelve ticks. Strengthened tests then reproduced this four-viewport regression: baseline #358 (run 40447682473) failed four of 76 new checks, with twelve ticks on an isolated 100ms stall. The two-consecutive-interval refinement passed candidate CI #359 (run 40447693466). Final reset-state candidate CI #360 (run 40447704725) passed **6,957/6,957** reported browser checks across **59** suites, including all **76/76** strengthened low-FPS checks. Independent promoted development-preview CI #361 (run 40448639934) independently passed **6,957/6,957**, all 59 suites, all four approved v3.9 audio sources byte-identical. Jobs and decoded logs were inspected.

## Release decision and next priorities
**BLOCKED.** This is deterministic headless Chromium timing/physics evidence, not independent physical Samsung or human acceptance. A real device at 10fps will still only display ten frames per second; this fix corrects the simulation's time scale, not hardware rendering cadence. The extra physics work may itself increase CPU pressure on a constrained device; that tradeoff is not measured on the Galaxy A37. Continue investigating actual Samsung weak shot feel, frame pacing and aiming with reproducible browser tests, preserving the current gameplay, and require independent physical-device/human acceptance plus explicit user approval before any production change. No monetisation, multiplayer, marketing or expansion.
