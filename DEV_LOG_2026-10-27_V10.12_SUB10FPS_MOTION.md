# Pool World v10.12 — sustained sub-10fps shot-motion time preservation
Date: 2026-10-27 UTC. Scope: development/poolworld-match-engine ONLY.

## Reproduced user-visible defect
The fixed-step 120Hz physics engine was capped at eight updates per animation callback unless two successive frame gaps were 75–125ms. Sustained 6–7fps gaps (~143–167ms) therefore never entered catch-up, making the balls travel in slow motion. At sustained 8fps, the former 15-step cap could not recover the first callback's seven missing updates; the shot lagged behind wall-clock time. These are deterministic Chromium timestamp fixtures, not measured Samsung A37 frame rates.

Mandatory regression test: `tests/test_pool60_sub10fps_motion.py` (commit `ae3f067a6bd89e05a03b63daa38fa14ebdbc45ff`), registered in `.github/workflows/poolworld-staging-tests.yml` (commit `3f67a15e99ffd6422745fae8124845fbde93c39f`). 96 browser checks across 390×844, 844×390, 768×1024 and 1366×768 viewports. They cover 6, 7, 8, 9, 15 and 60fps fixed-step pacing, persistent debt and bounded catch-up, isolated 125/160/200ms stalls, actual 8fps cue-ball position and velocity versus 120 manual physics steps, and browser errors.

### Final correction
- Staging gameplay commit `b682b2e1642ec98d90bfea86ce6ab5485dfd134b`, blob `315be966a704cd26c542554bb5059e718052c996`.
- After two consecutive 75–175ms frame gaps, budget up to 22 physics ticks (wall-time requirement plus two debt-recovery ticks); preserve the former eight-tick budget for isolated stalls.
- Increase the accumulator cap from 250ms to 300ms ONLY after sustained low FPS has been established. This prevents dropping 16.7ms of play time when a 6fps shot begins; idle frames and single hitches keep the old cap.
- Original calibrated shotSpeed, rolling, contact solver, six pockets, aiming, match logic, canvas visuals and four approved v3.9 audio URIs are unchanged.

## Limits and next work
This fixes a reproducible *simulation slow-motion* defect at extremely low callback rates. It does NOT make a 6–8fps display visually smooth, measure actual Samsung FPS or latency, prove improved shot strength at normal frame rates, or constitute independent physical-device/human acceptance. The user's Samsung A37 reports weak shots, jerky movement and difficult aiming; these remain release-blocking. Production requires independent Samsung/human acceptance and explicit user approval. Next: reproducible normal-FPS cue/ball visual continuity, input-to-visible latency, and shot-strength behavior; no expansion or monetisation.
