# Pool World v10.3 — swept grazing collision rescue (26 October 2026)

## Reproduced user-visible defect
At 120 Hz, a fast cue ball can enter and exit another ball's 24.4-unit
contact circle between physics updates. Both sampled endpoints are separated.
The old physics() checked only endpoint overlap and therefore transferred
**zero momentum**, although earliestCueContact() recorded firstHit=1.
This made a real grazing shot pass through an object ball while potentially
counting it as a legal first contact.

A concrete browser fixture uses an object at (400,285), cue initially
(397.57,309.35), and cue vx=9.75. Initial separation 24.47095 and final
separation ~24.47199 are both greater than 2*rad=24.4; the swept segment
crosses the contact circle. Baseline CI #310 (run 40412945644) failed 36
of 80 new checks across 390x844, 844x390, 768x1024, 1366x768,
including all swept cue-object momentum and object-object momentum fixtures.
The test-file-only CI #309 (40412924527) passed prior suites.

## Code change and safeguards
Added conservative swept AABB and quadratic entry-time detection to
POOL_WORLD_6.0_STAGING_MATCH.html. When a contact crosses the collision
circle but is separated at the end of the 120Hz tick, use its entry normal
to transfer the existing restitution/tangential impulse. Do not invent
penetration or reposition balls for these swept-only contacts. Keep the
existing overlap collision path, simultaneous-contact solver, energy limits,
shot power, pocket/jaw/cushion physics, match rules and four approved
v3.9 sound data URIs unchanged. Swept-only contacts are handled separately
from the simultaneous overlap batch to avoid silently skipping them.

Mandatory test tests/test_pool60_swept_grazing.py exercises 80 checks:
four viewport configurations; four cue grazing directions/sides each;
non-overlap at both endpoints, transferred momentum, firstHit validity,
finite nonincreasing energy; true near misses and object-object grazes.
